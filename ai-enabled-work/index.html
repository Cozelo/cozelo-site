Being asked to do more with less?

Every part of a firm is being asked to do more without adding people. AI can carry a real share of that work once five ingredients are in place, starting with knowledge.

For most teams, access to frontier models hasn’t delivered frontier results.

Today’s LLMs can deliver incredible work, but they need support to avoid generic responses.

The shape of AI-enabled work.

Whatever the work, the same five layers are needed for AI to deliver. Each builds on the one below it, starting from knowledge, with human judgement at the top.

  1. Layer 1

    Knowledge

    HumanAIRules-based

    The foundation: facts, definitions, priorities and the reasons behind them. Some of it is explicit and already written down. Much of it is implicit and lives in people’s heads.

  2. Layer 2

    Artificial Intelligence

    HumanAIRules-based

    The model that reasons over your knowledge. It weighs what matters, decides what to do next and drafts the work. It is only as good as the knowledge beneath it.

  3. Layer 3

    Execution & Scale

    HumanAIRules-based

    Where the work gets done. AI adds to the work people do rather than replacing it, and that addition is what lets a team do more without adding people.

    Human workRelationships, expertise and the calls only your people can make.
    AI and rules-based workAgents and automated workflows that add capacity on a schedule or on demand.
  4. Layer 4

    Oversight

    HumanAIRules-based

    Checks on the work before a person sees it. AI reviews drafts against your standards, and rules-based checks catch anything missing or out of policy. Work that falls short goes back for rework.

  5. Layer 5

    Judgement

    HumanAIRules-based

    The final call. A person approves the work or sends it back to the layer that needs to fix it. As AI improves, oversight can take on more of the checking. The decision stays with people.

AI should work like an expert, not an intern.

Without your firm’s knowledge, AI works like a bright new intern: fluent, quick and generic. With it, AI can reason like your best people. That knowledge comes in two kinds.

Explicit knowledgeAlready written down: services, pricing, client records, policies and past proposals. Usually scattered across systems.
Implicit knowledgeLives in people’s heads: why clients choose you, how calls get made and what good looks like. It has to be drawn out and written down.

Brought together, they form what specialists call an ontology. It covers four things.

  • DefinitionsWhat your terms mean, what you offer and who counts as an ideal client.
  • PrioritiesWhat matters most, what comes first and what to leave alone.
  • DecisionsHow calls get made, by whom, and what good looks like.
  • ReasonsWhy clients choose you, and why you work the way you do.

Then redesign the work around all five ingredients.

Adding AI to one step makes that step faster and leaves the rest as it was. The gain comes from redesigning the whole operating model around one shared base of knowledge.

Digitizing to unlock value.

For more than 50 years firms have been digitizing information to unlock value. In 2025, more than $300 billion was spent to manage and capture everything from operations data in Excel spreadsheets to relationship data in Salesforce.

While firms are focused on which AI models, agents and tools to use, what most often holds them back from finding scale and value is the step before: preparing the firm’s knowledge for AI to use.
