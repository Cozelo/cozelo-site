Start with the outcome.

We help your team put AI to work on a specific job, then build what it needs to deliver: the knowledge, the workflow and the checks. Five steps, with the same people throughout.

Five steps to AI-enabled work.

Every step works through the same five layers: knowledge, artificial intelligence, execution and scale, oversight and judgment. Each builds on the one below it.

The shape of AI-enabled work
  1. 1

    Understand the outcome

    Where do you want AI to help?

    We start with a job to be done, not an AI solution looking for a problem. Together we agree what success looks like and how we’ll measure it.

  2. 2

    Map how the work happens today

    How is your team working, with AI, to deliver that outcome now?

    We trace the work through each layer: the knowledge it draws on, the AI and tools in use, who does what, how it’s checked and who makes the final call. That map is the baseline.

  3. 3

    Choose what to fix first

    What does your team need for AI to deliver the outcome?

    We rank the gaps by their effect on the outcome. The first is usually knowledge: how your firm decides and why clients choose you, never written down for AI to use.

  4. 4

    Build the foundation and the workflow

    How does AI fit with how your team is built and works?

    We capture the knowledge, connect it to the AI tools your team already uses, and redesign the whole workflow around it: what people do, what AI and automation do, the checks before work reaches a person, and where people make the call. Your team works in it from day one.

  5. 5

    Measure and refine

    What results is the new workflow delivering, and how can it improve?

    We track results against the measures set in step one, keep the knowledge current and hand more of the checking to oversight as AI earns trust. The decision stays with people.

The same minds, throughout.

The people who map how your team works are the people who build the new workflow and stay to refine it. Strategy and build stay together, because every gap in how work gets done starts at a handoff.
